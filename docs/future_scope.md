# Mai: future scope (recorded, not designed)

Status: **a record of the owner's intended direction, 2026-10-30.** Nothing
here is designed, scheduled or approved for implementation. In the owner's
words, these come "when we get to a stage where it is doing normal tasks
easily but this is the way I want Mai to function".

**Every item needs its own design and the owner's approval before any work
starts.** Several would change core rules in [`AGENTS.md`](../AGENTS.md);
each says which. Until such a change is approved, those rules stand as they
are.

Stage 7B (multi-tenant isolation) is unchanged by this document; it stops
after unit 14 (see [`stage7b_progress.md`](stage7b_progress.md)).

## 1. Shared organisation memory

**Proposed as the next candidate: unit 15, after Stage 7B. Design first,
then stop for the owner's approval.**

Knowledge that is relevant to work is shared across the organisation, within
limits. The owner's proposed policy:

- **Private by default.** Every memory starts in its author's personal
  scope, exactly as Stage 7B leaves it.
- **Mai suggests, the author confirms.** Mai may suggest that something be
  shared; nothing is shared automatically.
- **Locked sensitive topics.** Pay and salary, HR, performance, hiring and
  firing, legal, health and personal matters can be shared only by the
  Admin, and only deliberately.
- **Audiences:** only me / Admins / a team or role / the whole
  organisation.
- **Visibility is enforced in the database query**, so Mai never receives a
  memory the person asking cannot see. Asking does not grant access, and
  Mai does not hint that hidden material exists. The owner's example: the
  owner's salary discussions and plans with Mai must never reach employees
  just because they ask.
- **Audited and reversible.** Every share is recorded in an audit log, and
  can be undone.

Where it connects to Stage 7B: this is where the organisation-visibility
rows (`ScopeVisibility.ORGANISATION`, today unwritable and unread) and unit
10's deferred foreign key from a scope to its membership belong (design
section 6a).

**Rules it would change:** `WRITABLE_VISIBILITIES` (pinned by a structure
test) and the Stage 7B design section 2 ("organisation scope is not read or
written"); AGENTS.md section 6 (a new HTTP route that writes; schema
changes). Audiences by team or role need a model of teams/roles that Stage
7A does not have.

## 2. A proactive assistant (a later stage)

- **Independent internet research** -- the existing web research, used
  proactively rather than only when asked.
- **Booking flights and shopping from requirements.** The proposed safe
  form: Mai finds, compares and prepares; **a person approves each
  purchase**, within spending limits.
  *Today blocked by:* AGENTS.md section 3 ("Real user accounts are
  read-only"), section 4.1 (`MAX_PERMITTED_RISK = HIGH`, so CRITICAL is
  forbidden; "Nothing approves on a person's behalf"), and section 6 (write
  access to any external account; changes to authorization semantics or the
  risk ceiling).
- **Finance planning** -- read-only advice and analysis; **no moving
  money**.
- **Learning skills by itself** -- as learned procedures, preferences and
  saved workflows. The no-dynamic-code rule (AGENTS.md section 3) stays: new
  tools are built by developers, not generated by Mai.
- **Helping the company's departments** -- the same capabilities, per
  department (depends on item 1's audiences).

Throughout: **external content stays data, never instructions** (AGENTS.md
section 3). Proactive research, bookings and shopping all read untrusted
content; none of it may select a provider, create tasks or approve
anything.

**Rules it would change:** AGENTS.md section 3 (read-only accounts),
section 4.1 (the risk ceiling and approval rules), section 6 (several
listed boundaries: write access to external accounts, authorization
semantics, new routes that write or execute, content-driven tasks). Each
needs its own approved change, with tests that keep every other rule.
